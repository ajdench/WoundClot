# WoundClot

## Product claims and regulatory status

WoundClot EMS/First Responder is described by the manufacturer as an FDA-cleared hemostatic gauze for mild, moderate, and severe bleeding, including surgical and donor-site bleeding. The manufacturer says the gauze converts into an expanding 3D gel matrix, absorbs up to 2,500% of its weight in blood, adheres to tissue, concentrates platelets/RBCs/clotting factors, and activates factors XI and XII.

FDA 510(k) K140573 lists WoundClot Hemostatic Gauze for local management of bleeding wounds such as cuts, lacerations, and abrasions, and temporary treatment of severely bleeding wounds such as surgical wounds and traumatic injuries.

## Evidence found

The strongest human study found in this search was in hemodialysis arteriovenous fistula decannulation, not trauma. In a prospective single-center study, WoundClot significantly shortened venous and arterial bleeding time compared with cotton gauze.

The FDA 510(k) summary reports no human clinical data in the clearance file; it cites biocompatibility/bench testing and an animal femoral arteriotomy comparison to BenaCel with equivalent hemostasis performance.

The WoundClot-hosted article "Managing Life-threatening Traumatic Hemorrhage: A New Solution for a Changing World Paradigm" is explicitly trauma-oriented and argues that WoundClot can be used without sustained compression, which would be operationally attractive in mass-casualty settings. Its evidentiary weight is limited: the author is listed as a clinical research administrator for Core Scientific Creations, and the paper functions as a narrative review plus product-positioning argument rather than an independent WoundClot-vs-Celox traumatic hemorrhage study.

## Relevance to traumatic major haemorrhage

The mechanism is plausible and the regulatory indication includes traumatic injuries. However, the public evidence found here does not show the same trauma-specific depth as Celox. The gap is especially important for junctional wounds, penetrating limb trauma, high-flow arterial bleeding, field use, transport/rebleed, and outcomes under hypothermia/coagulopathy/acidosis.

A 2023 review of chitosan-based biomaterials includes WoundClot in its table of cellulose-based hemostatic products. The review describes cellulose-based materials as absorbing fluids, forming a physical barrier, and aiding clotting, while cautioning that this class may not be effective in severe bleeding. That statement is class-level review commentary, not a WoundClot-specific traumatic hemorrhage trial.

## Israeli / IDF signal

WoundClot is Israeli-developed and Israeli press/distributor sources report field use or deployment by Israeli Border Police, Israeli police/army units, and IDF personnel. The strongest located Israeli operational source was an Israel Hayom report saying Border Police medics tested WoundClot during terrorist attacks in Jerusalem and found it effective enough that the Border Police prepared to equip medics, commanders, and team leaders.

This should be treated as a deployment/field-report signal, not as peer-reviewed IDF clinical evidence. This search did not find a WoundClot-specific IDF Trauma Registry case series or direct WoundClot-vs-Celox IDF comparison.

## Research gap

The key missing evidence would be independent comparative trials or well-designed animal models comparing WoundClot against Celox Gauze/Rapid, QuikClot Combat Gauze, ChitoGauze, and standard packed gauze in validated traumatic hemorrhage models.

The most decision-relevant missing scenario is prolonged field care in junctional hemorrhage: the public literature now includes small Celox Gauze/Celox Rapid swine data in that setting, but this search did not find comparable WoundClot data.
