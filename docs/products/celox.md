# Celox

## Product claims and regulatory status

Celox is a chitosan-based hemostatic product family. FDA materials for Celox Rapid X-Ray Gauze describe a sterile, single-use external hemostatic gauze coated with chitosan-based granules. The device forms a gel-like plug/coagulum at the bleeding site with manual pressure.

The FDA indication for Celox Rapid X-Ray detectable Z-fold hemostatic gauze is temporary external use to control moderate to severe bleeding, and temporary external use for bleeding of lacerations, minor cuts, and abrasions.

## Evidence found

Celox has a stronger trauma evidence footprint than WoundClot:

- A randomized clinical trial in civilian penetrating limb trauma found Celox-coated gauze reduced time to hemostasis and blood loss compared with conventional pressure dressing.
- A randomized, controlled swine femoral arteriotomy no-hold model found Celox Rapid had lower secondary blood loss and shorter packing time than standard gauze or Combat Gauze.
- A swine model comparing novel hemostatic dressings with QuikClot Combat Gauze found Celox Gauze had numerically higher survival than Combat Gauze, although not statistically significant in pairwise comparison.
- A 2026 swine prolonged-field-care pilot found Combat Gauze, Celox Gauze, Celox Rapid, and Veriset met a 2-hour survival threshold in fatal junctional hemorrhage. Celox Gauze and Celox Rapid each achieved 60% 24-hour survival in the small sample.
- A systematic review of prehospital hemostatic dressings found the evidence base dominated by Celox, QuikClot Combat Gauze, and HemCon, with no demonstrable difference in effectiveness among commonly used products.
- A prehospital hemorrhage-control narrative review summarizes current chitosan generations including Celox Rapid and Celox Gauze, notes large-animal testing mostly against Combat Gauze, and cites Celox clinical case reports plus the civilian penetrating-trauma RCT.
- A 2023 chitosan biomaterials review lists Celox among chitosan-based hemostatic products and describes the class mechanism as positive-charge interaction with negatively charged blood components, platelet activation/agglutination, blood-protein aggregation, and wet-tissue barrier formation.
- TCCC 2024 lists Celox Gauze as an alternative hemostatic adjunct for compressible external hemorrhage not amenable to limb tourniquet use, while Combat Gauze remains the dressing of choice.

## Relevance to traumatic major haemorrhage

Celox is not uniquely proven superior to every established hemostatic gauze. The literature remains limited and product generation matters. But compared with WoundClot, Celox has much more directly relevant evidence for prehospital and traumatic bleeding.

## Israeli / IDF signal

This search did not find IDF-specific Celox outcome evidence. The IDF-specific literature found here centers on QuikClot Combat Gauze, especially the 122-patient IDF Trauma Registry case series. Celox remains supported by other trauma evidence streams, but not by a located IDF-specific series.
